Every application tracks itself

A tracker you never update by hand. Your mailbox already knows every application you sent, every auto-acknowledgement, every invitation and every rejection — it is just unstructured. Loop reads those signals, resolves them into one application per real job, and derives the pipeline instead of asking you to maintain it.

01

Where truth comes from

Scraping the job boards themselves is the obvious idea and the wrong one: it needs your credentials, breaks on every layout change, is against most terms of service, and LinkedIn's "My jobs" list does not know that a recruiter replied. The high-signal, low-maintenance surface is the one place every platform must reach you — your inbox — plus the calendar it writes to.

primary 0.7–0.98
Mailbox

Gmail API history + IMAP IDLE. Auto-acks, recruiter threads, rejections, offer letters. Covers every platform and every private career page for free.

primary 0.95
Calendar

Every .ics invite from a company domain is a near-certain interview, with its exact stage often in the title. The cheapest stage detector that exists.

fallback 1.0
Quick add

Share-sheet from the job posting URL, or two taps in the app. Fills the gap for applications that never generate an email at all.

rejected —
Portal crawling

Credential custody, CAPTCHA, ToS risk, constant breakage — for a status field that lags the email by days. Kept out of v1 on purpose.

System map

Seven small services, one Postgres, one queue. They are separate because they fail and scale differently — a mailbox connector holding long-lived OAuth tokens should not share a process with an LLM call that can hang for thirty seconds — not because microservices are fashionable. All of them fit on one free-tier box.

03

One message, end to end

A recruiter at Fastweb replies to an application you sent through LinkedIn eleven days ago. Nothing in that email says "LinkedIn", and the company's ATS is Greenhouse. This is the path it takes.

t+0s
Gmail push arrives

The webhook carries a history id, not content. Connector pulls the delta, stores message_id + sha256, publishes raw.message. A replayed webhook is a no-op.

t+0.3s
Classify, then strip

Sender domain is a known ATS vendor, so heuristics are skipped. HTML reduced to text; quoted history, signatures and tracking pixels dropped.

t+0.4s
Extract by template

The Greenhouse rule yields company, role and intent: schedule_screening deterministically. No model call, no cost, ~4 ms.

t+0.5s
Resolve

New thread, so: canonical domain + role embedding at cosine 0.91 against an application created 11 days ago from a LinkedIn confirmation. Above 0.82 → same application, second source attached.

t+0.6s
Append, fold

One row: stage_advanced → hr_screen, confidence 0.94, evidence = message id. Nothing is overwritten, so a wrong guess is corrected by appending.

t+0.9s
Surfaces update

SSE pushes to any open client, the nudge service cancels the "silent 11 days" reminder and schedules "prepare screening". Your phone buzzes once.

04

The extraction ladder

Extraction decides whether this thing works, and it is the only part that could quietly cost money. So it is a ladder: each rung is cheaper and more certain than the one below, and a message only falls through when the rung above abstains.

1
ATS template registry

Greenhouse, Lever, Workday, Ashby, SmartRecruiters, Workable, iCIMS, Taleo, Recruitee, BambooHR — plus LinkedIn and Indeed's own confirmations. Sender domain + subject pattern → declarative YAML rule. One rule covers thousands of companies.

78%
of messages · 4 ms · €0
2
Calendar & thread heuristics

An .ics attachment or a meeting link from a company domain resolves the stage by itself. A reply on a known thread inherits the application with no parsing at all.

13%
of messages · 2 ms · €0
3
Local model, structured output

Unknown template, human-written email, or Italian/English mixed prose. A 3–7B instruct model behind an OpenAI-compatible endpoint, called with a JSON schema and a 1500-token cap. Slow (2–5 s) but queued, so nobody waits.

8%
of messages · 2–5 s · €0
4
Ask the human, once

Confidence below 0.6, or two candidate applications within 0.05 of each other. Lands in an inbox-shaped review queue: one tap to confirm, and the answer is written back as a new template rule or a resolver alias.

1%
2 taps · teaches rung 1

At 25 live applications the system sees on the order of 40 relevant messages a week, of which three reach the model. Even on a paid API that is cents per month — the local model exists so the number is exactly zero and no email body ever leaves the box.

Decision · rung 3

Which engine runs the model

The extractor never talks to a model directly — it talks to an OpenAI-compatible /v1/chat/completions endpoint with a JSON schema attached. That makes the engine a config line rather than an architectural commitment, which matters because this workload has two very different shapes: a trickle of three messages a day, and one enormous backfill.

LLAMA.CPP default

Steady state. A Q4 GGUF of a 7B fits in ~5 GB, ARM kernels are first-class, and the model unloads when idle — which is 99% of the time. Three requests a day never need batching.

~5 GB · idles at zero · one container
vLLM batch profile

Yes — and it earns its place twice: the 12-month backfill in phase 1, where continuous batching turns hours into minutes, and phase 4, where many users' messages arrive at once. Guided decoding gives a harder JSON guarantee than format:json does.

on demand · not always-on
HOSTED API escape hatch

Same interface, one env var. Opt-in per user, because it moves email text off the box — the one thing sheet 09 promises it will not do by default.

off by default
The catch on the free ARM box

vLLM's CPU backend does run on aarch64 with NEON — pre-built Arm wheels exist now, and oneDNN + Arm Compute Library kernels come on automatically — but it serves FP32/FP16/BF16, so a 7B is ~15 GB resident rather than 5, and it holds a preallocated KV cache instead of unloading. On a 24 GB Ampere instance that is affordable; as an always-on service for three requests a day it is the wrong trade.

So: both, behind one switch

docker compose --profile batch up brings vLLM online, the extractor's queue drains through it, and it stops. Steady state stays on llama.cpp. The only requirement this puts on the design is that rung 3 be stateless and idempotent per message — which it already is, because everything downstream is replayable from seen_messages.

Domain model

One aggregate — the application — with an append-only event log as its only source of truth. Everything the interface shows, including every statistic, is a fold over that log. Sources are many-to-one on purpose: the same job found on LinkedIn and on the company site is one application with two provenances, which is what makes channel statistics honest.

Why event-sourced

Every statistic you asked for is a time question — days to first response, median time in stage, seasonal averages. A current-state table cannot answer any of them retroactively. The log can, and it is also how a better extractor next month gets to re-derive last month's history.

Event types

applied · acknowledged · stage_advanced · interview_scheduled · interview_held · rejected · withdrawn · offer_received · offer_negotiated · accepted · went_silent · human_corrected · note_added

Deliberate omission

No email bodies in the database. An event references a mailbox message id; the app deep-links to the real thread rather than re-hosting your correspondence.

Silence is a state, not a stage

Most applications end by being ignored. After a stage-specific timeout the application is flagged dormant — visible in stats as a ghost rate, hidden from the daily view, never silently deleted.

Stages skip and go back

Transitions are not a strict chain. A referral can start at technical; a company can send you back a round. The fold takes the highest-confidence latest event, and any monotonicity assumption is a bug.

Your correction always wins

A human_corrected event carries confidence 1.0 and pins the stage until a later signal of equal confidence. The agent is never allowed to argue with you twice.

08

The agent that suggests

A scheduled fold over the log, not a chat. Each rule produces at most one suggestion per application, expires on its own, and is written so that doing nothing is always acceptable.

follow-up duestage dwell > p75 of your own history

"Nexi asked for your availability 6 days ago and you replied. Your median wait at this stage is 3 days." One tap drafts a three-line follow-up in the thread's language, which you edit and send from your own mail client.

prepareinterview in < 48h

Surfaces the posting, the thread, your notes on this company, and what the previous round asked. No advice generation — just everything you already wrote, in one place, at the right hour.

deadlinetake-home due date parsed

The only hard alert in the system, because it is the only one where silence has a cost you cannot undo. Fires at 72h and 12h.

let it gosilence > 2× stage p90

Offers to archive as dormant, in one tap, batched weekly. This is the rule that keeps the pipeline honest — and the daily view short — without you having to admit defeat one card at a time.

Budget: at most three suggestions surfaced at once, at most one push notification a day, none between 21:00 and 08:00. The tracker's job is to lower the ambient anxiety of a job search, and a tracker that notifies you nine times raises it.

09

Credentials, secrets, GDPR

This system reads your mailbox. That is the whole design risk, and it is the reason the architecture is self-hosted first: the trust boundary is a box you own. If it is ever opened to other people, the guarantees below become promises you have to keep — so they are built in from v1 rather than retrofitted.

Minimum viable scopes

gmail.readonly and calendar.readonly, nothing more — no send, no modify, no contacts. Loop drafts follow-ups but never has the right to send one. IMAP accounts use app-specific passwords, never your main password.

Envelope encryption

Per-user data key (libsodium secretbox) wrapping tokens and any stored snippet; DEKs wrapped by a KEK held outside the database — env var on a single-tenant box, KMS or an age-encrypted file if hosted. A stolen dump yields nothing readable.

Retention by default

Raw message text lives in memory for the length of one extraction, and on disk only if you opt into a 7-day debug buffer. What persists is the extracted fields, a message id and a hash. Attachments are referenced, not copied.

Data subject rights, as endpoints

GET /export returns the full event log as JSON plus a CSV of applications. DELETE /account is a real cascade including the queue, the projections and the vector index, with a receipt. Articles 15 and 17 satisfied by construction, not by a support inbox.

Third parties: none, by choice

Local inference means no processor agreement, no transfer outside the EU, no clause about model training. If a hosted model is ever enabled it becomes an explicit per-user toggle with a named processor, off by default.

Special-category caution

Recruitment mail can carry health, disability or diversity data. The extractor has an explicit deny-list of fields it will never persist, and the model prompt forbids returning them. This also keeps the multi-user version out of Article 9 territory.

If it becomes multi-tenant

Row-level security on user_id from day one, so the shared-instance version is a deployment flag rather than a rewrite. Google OAuth verification and a CASA assessment are the real cost of that step — plan months, not a weekend.

10

Running it for nothing

Seven services on one always-free ARM instance, behind Caddy, orchestrated by Docker Compose. Postgres does queue, cron and vector search, so there is nothing else to operate. The whole thing idles at a few hundred megabytes.

Why not serverless

IMAP IDLE and a renewed Gmail watch both want a long-lived process, and local inference wants resident RAM. One small box is cheaper and simpler than working around a function timeout.

Failure posture

Everything downstream of the connector is replayable from seen_messages, and the connector catches up from a stored history id. The box can be down for a day and lose nothing but timeliness.

Cost of growth

The first bottleneck at multi-user scale is inference, not the database. That is when rung 3 moves to a batched or hosted model — and when the free-tier promise ends, so it is also when you charge.
11

Build order

Each phase is usable alone, and each earns the right to build the next. The riskiest assumption — that email extraction is accurate enough to trust — is tested in phase 1, on your own real inbox, before any interface work.

P0 · weekend
Schema + manual

Event log, stage machine, quick-add, list and detail screens. Already better than a spreadsheet.

P1 · the risk
Read the mailbox

Connector, classifier, ten ATS templates, resolver. Backfill 12 months of your own inbox and measure precision against what you remember.

P2
Calendar + review

Interview detection, the model rung, and the two-tap confirmation loop that teaches the registry.

P3
Statistics + nudges

Projections, the funnel, channel and timing views, the four nudge rules, web push.

P4 · optional
Open it up

RLS on, OAuth verification, onboarding, hosted inference, billing. A different product with a different burden.

Open questions for you: which mail providers you actually use, whether the take-home deadline alert may break quiet hours, and whether dormant applications should ever leave the funnel denominator.
